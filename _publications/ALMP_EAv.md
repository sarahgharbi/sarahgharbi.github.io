title: "When Does Subsidized Employment Work? Unpacking the Small Average Effects of Youth Employment Programs"
collection: publications
permalink: /publication/ALMP_EAv
excerpt: 'We investigate when subsidized employment generates lasting gains for disadvantaged youth using France’s Emplois d’avenir program. Linking program contracts to longitudinal administrative data, we estimate effects using a staggered difference-in-differences design with regression adjustment and use principal strata and Lee-type bounds to distinguish job creation from effects on participants who would have worked anyway. Six years after entry, the program raises employment by 6.4 percentage points and annual earnings by about €660, with substantially larger gains for older entrants facing weaker outside options. About three quarters of participants would have been employed anyway, while 6 percent remain employed because of the program. Persistent earnings gains are driven by these program-created jobs; among those who would have worked anyway, we find no earning gains. Mediation analysis shows that the program increases access to cognitively demanding jobs more clearly for men, although their earnings payoff is substantially larger for women. The program also raises parenthood by about 4 percentage points for both genders, whose asymmetric earnings consequences help explain why substantial gender-specific mechanisms translate into only a small overall gender gap in program effects.'
